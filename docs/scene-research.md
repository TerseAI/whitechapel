# Places, daily life and visual references

These sources support historical setting and visual research. A source may establish an address or describe one furnishing without documenting a whole building. Record that distinction when authoring a scene; use the [geography contract](geography/README.md) for map registration.

## Buck’s Row

PC John Neil’s inquest testimony describes a closed stable gateway about nine or ten feet high, houses to its east, the board school to its west, and Essex Wharf opposite. These are useful spatial relationships; they do not provide a complete measured street elevation. [Daily Telegraph, 3 September 1888](https://www.casebook.org/press_reports/daily_telegraph/dt880903/).

Neil described darkness near the discovery; later reporters disagreed about illumination. Do not infer a verified lamp plan from a general account of darkness. [Evening News, 7 September 1888](https://www.casebook.org/press_reports/evening_news/18880907.html).

The [Kelly & Co. 1888 directory plan](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Buck%27s_Row_plan_Kelly_1888.jpg) helps with the street network. It does not establish building heights or interiors.

## Common lodging houses

Emily Holland gave her address as 18 Thrawl Street and said Nichols had shared her room. This supports the address and communal setting, without establishing where a table, staircase or hearth stood. [Daily Telegraph, 4 September 1888](https://www.casebook.org/press_reports/daily_telegraph/dt880904/).

An 1886 illustration of a Spitalfields common lodging-house kitchen shows a hearth, domestic utensils and hanging clothes. It is a reference for a type of room, not a verified view of No. 18. [Catalogued illustration](https://www.magnoliabox.com/products/the-kitchen-of-a-common-lodging-house-spitalfields-london-uk-1886-ql-140609-1207).

Goldsmid’s account describes a low-roofed common kitchen furnished with tables, benches, a range and fires. His description concerns another lodging house. Use it for period furnishings, with its location and viewpoint in mind. [Howard J. Goldsmid, Dottings of a Dosser, chapter 2](https://victorianlondon.org/publications6/dosser-02.htm).

## Shoemaking and leather work

Northampton Museums’ surviving Victorian shoemaker’s bench (1860–90) combines a low seat with compartments for tools, lasts, nails and leather. Its account describes small home workshops and the need for light and heat for glue and wax. [Shoemaker’s Bench](https://timeline.northamptonmuseums.com/timeline/shoemakers-bench).

The Shoemakers Museum describes hand methods, lasts and tools used in cottage workshops before factory production. [Making Shoes](https://shoemakersmuseum.org.uk/collection/in-the-archive/making-shoes/). A period illustration depicts a seated shoemaker among his tools and lasts. [The Huntington collection](https://www.huntington.org/collections/lib-p16003coll4-3421).

These sources support a trade setting. They do not establish the layout of a particular historical person’s workshop.

## Hanbury Street

Chandler’s and Phillips’s inquest evidence describes the passage, rear steps, dividing fence and wooden shed. These relationships help reconstruct access and sightlines. Building proportions, paving patterns and incidental domestic objects require separate support or an explicit artistic interpretation. [Daily News, 14 September 1888](https://www.casebook.org/press_reports/daily_news/18880914/?printer=true).

Long said she judged the time by hearing the brewer’s clock in Brick Lane strike half past five before entering Hanbury Street. Attribute that timing to her account when using it. [Daily Telegraph, 20 September 1888](https://www.casebook.org/press_reports/daily_telegraph/dt880920/).

## Commercial Street police station

Historic England describes the surviving 1874–75 station: red brick, stone dressings, a channelled masonry ground floor, sash windows with glazing bars, and a panelled central door with a radial fanlight. [Former Police Station, list entry 1065207](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1065207).

The listed exterior does not document a charge desk, shelving, room plan or other interior arrangement. A scene using such details needs additional research or should be described as an interpretation.

| George Lusk | [Portrait reproduction](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:George_Lusk,_President_of_the_Whitechapel_Vigilance_Committee.jpg) | Check the image’s provenance before treating it as a likeness reference. |
| Francis Tumblety | His [1872 narrative](https://www.casebook.org/ripper_media/rps.tumblety.1872_book/) discusses conspicuous clothing and a black moustache. | It is an earlier self-description, not a verified outfit at the time of the murders. |

An invented appearance is not evidence of guilt. A police role can support a uniform silhouette without establishing an individual likeness. Linked museum and commercial reproductions are research references; redistribution needs its own rights check.
