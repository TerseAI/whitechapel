# Mystery in Whitechapel

The current working script for **Whitechapel**, an investigation for two human players intended to take about an hour. This document contains the plot, scene choices, evidence flow, progression and references. The playable story, dialogue branches and artwork live in stories/sixth-murder. This working plot describes the current candlestick-slip version.

## The story

Whitechapel, 16–17 November 1888. Inspector Reed and Inspector Ellis have been temporarily assigned from elsewhere in London to assist the Whitechapel murder enquiry after Mary Kelly’s death. Constable Hale has arranged rooms at the Lantern. They arrive in plain clothes, keep their occupation private and meet Nora Vale, a needleworker leaving for work arranged by her sister in Bristol.

The next morning, while the detectives report to Commercial Street, George Moss arrives for Nora’s luggage and finds her dead in the rear yard. Hale summons Dr William Alden, records the discovery and arranges removal to the mortuary before the detectives return. Nora’s injuries resemble the recent murders. Arthur Vale introduces himself as her brother, seeks her belongings and directs suspicion towards George.

The players establish that Nora was attacked in her room and moved in her travelling trunk. They uncover the marriage Arthur concealed: Nora had left him, and he came to make her return. The room’s missing candlestick is recovered from George’s cart. The fatal injury is a blunt blow to the head. The knife wounds were inflicted after death, and the knife was left openly beside Nora; Hale holds it. Arthur later volunteers that the missing candlestick was under the sacks in George’s cart, although the recovery was kept private. A partly burned newspaper gives the players a way to test the apparent connection with the Whitechapel murders.

Arthur killed Nora after she refused to come home. He imitated the reported injuries and arranged her body in the yard to make her death look like another of the murders. The final interview lets players dismantle his account, choose how far to pursue his admissions and decide when to submit their joint report. Their evidence supports the case while he maintains his denial of the killing.

The solution is fixed; the investigation and conversations have different routes. Nora, Arthur, this crime and the Lantern are invented. Solving Nora’s murder does not identify the historical murderer.

## How this script plays

Two players choose different detectives before the game starts. Reed is short and stocky with a magnificent moustache; Ellis is very tall with prominent sideburns. They need no elaborate backstories. Either can inspect objects, question witnesses and propose findings.

Every scene below specifies what players can inspect, what they can ask, how responses differ and what resolves its questions. Quoted prompts are proposed player-facing lines. Short witness replies illustrate the direction of a branch; they are not a continuous performance to play automatically.

- Players choose topics and then a clearly worded response or evidence challenge. A selected exchange normally lasts two to six subtitle turns before control returns. Optional follow-ups add detail without making everyone hear every line.
- An approach can change what a witness volunteers, which explanation they adopt, or which follow-up becomes available. Several approaches may succeed. A successful interview result means the selected enquiry has been answered and recorded; it does not certify that the witness told the truth.
- Unsupported accusations receive a specific response. Players keep their discoveries, can ask another question, inspect more evidence and retry. A completed successful topic replays its recorded result; a changed question is a separate follow-up topic.
- Objects exist from the first visit to their location. Players can find the candlestick before hearing it is missing, once Hale has secured a private search. Burned clothing and newspaper are available when the search stage opens. Finding an object and establishing its significance are separate actions.
- Evidence and replies are shared. One investigator controls a witness interview at a time. The partner can inspect another object, question another witness or consult the Case Map. Closing the interview releases it so the other player can take the next topic. The framework does not automatically alternate speakers or make every response a joint vote.
- Questions, connections and findings belong on the Case Map. Both players must agree on chapter conclusions. They may disagree about a theory and keep investigating before submitting it.
- Cutscenes establish arrival, a change of time or a short departure. They do not conduct the investigation or deliver Arthur’s confrontation. Allow roughly 15–30 seconds for each transition, with the existing joint skip and acknowledgement controls.

The numbered scenes are a reading order, not nine locked levels. Scenes 1–5 share an open enquiry stage. Nora’s room and the inn witnesses are available on return; Hale’s account identifies the mortuary, and Baines or Nora’s work correspondence identifies the workshop. A witness never waits for an arbitrary chapter number to answer a question the players can already support.

### Progression and approximate pacing

| Engine stage | Playable scenes | Joint completion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Arrival | Opening — 5 minutes | **Retire for the evening**, after the lodging, Nora’s plans and collection arrangement are established. |
| Enquiry | Scenes 1–5 — 32 minutes combined | Submit the supported finding about Arthur’s visit, concealed relationship and movement of the trunk. Hale takes him to the station and a search is arranged. |
| Search | Scene 6 — 8 minutes | **Return to Commercial Street**, after recovering the objects and completing the required comparisons. Earlier enquiry locations remain available for missed evidence or follow-ups. |
| Confrontation | Scene 7 — 12 minutes | Both submit the supported final report after addressing Arthur’s principal accounts. The medical comparison, unshared-detail deduction and checked officer explanation are required; further movement and staging admissions are optional. |
| Closing | Return to the Lantern — 3 minutes | **Close the enquiry**, after deciding what to say to the people waiting there. |

These are targets including inspection, choices, reading and discussion, not measured playtime. Optional branches and recovery can extend a session. The confrontation budget belongs to player decisions and short exchanges; it is not twelve minutes of uninterrupted dialogue.

## Why the detectives are staying at the inn

Abberline needs help checking the witness accounts arriving after Miller’s Court. Reed and Ellis normally work elsewhere in London. Temporary rooms keep them near Commercial Street and the witnesses they need to visit. Hale knows Baines through his district work and reserves two front rooms without disclosing their occupation. The Lantern is chosen before anyone knows Nora is in danger.

The assignment is read privately before the opening. Proposed order:

> You are temporarily assigned to the Whitechapel murder enquiry. We need additional officers to follow up the witness accounts received since the murder in Miller’s Court.
>
> Constable Hale has arranged rooms for you at the Lantern. Mrs Baines is expecting Mr Reed and Mr Ellis this evening. She has not been told your occupation. Wear plain clothes and keep your assignment private while lodging there, so that your arrival does not become common talk.
>
> Report to me at Commercial Street tomorrow morning for your assignments.
>
> F. Abberline  
> Inspector

They use their real surnames and ordinary clothes. Baines learns they are inspectors only when they return to investigate Nora’s death. Their posting and accommodation are fictional elements of this case.

## Opening — The evening before

**Place:** Lantern common room and visible rear service route. **Player purpose:** Settle in and meet the people whose arrangements will matter tomorrow.

Baines greets the expected lodgers. Nora finishes a repair to Baines’s sleeve at the table. George has delivered coal and is arranging the morning luggage collection. The detectives can approach them in any order; each conversation supplies its own context.

**Objects and documents:** Abberline’s order; sewing case and sleeve; Nora’s travelling trunk; common-room newspaper; George’s cart beside the service entrance; the heavy candlestick Baines takes towards Nora’s room. Its bent handle and blue wax make it recognizable later. These are ordinary belongings in use, not a row of highlighted murder clues.

| Player prompt or action | Response and resulting route |
| --- | --- |
| Ask Baines: “Hale reserved rooms for Reed and Ellis.” | “I’ve kept the front rooms.” She explains breakfast and the rear passage. If approached later, she recognizes them without replaying the introduction. |
| Ask Nora: “Are you travelling tomorrow?” | She explains Bristol, her sister’s offer of sewing work and her uncertainty about the new job. Record the plans so later dialogue can recall something actually heard. |
| Ask Nora: “What are you making?” | She shows the repair and talks about her work. A follow-up about the travelling things reaches the Bristol account. This route gives more time with Nora before the same necessary information. |
| Ask George: “Are you taking her to the station?” | He explains that he is a cart driver hired to carry the trunk. Nora will travel separately. Baines arranged the paid job. |
| Ask George: “Will you load it tonight?” | “I’ll leave the cart here tonight. Bring the horse round in the morning, then we’ll load her trunk.” Baines asks him to leave the steps clear. |
| Examine the newspaper or ask about it | The fictional report describes throat and abdominal injuries; its illustration includes a scarf across the depicted woman’s face. Nora asks what the detectives think of the report, then talks about walking home with a colleague. They keep their occupation private. It remains readable in the morning if skipped now. |

The sleeve is finished before Nora retires, whichever topic the players chose. Baines can explain the luggage route while showing the rooms. The two inspectors can split the introductions; shared records prevent a requirement that both personally hear every exchange.

**Completion:** Record the lodging, Nora’s Bristol plans and George’s collection/cart arrangement, then both choose **Retire for the evening**. No deduction or accusation is required. A brief transition establishes that they leave by the front door early the next morning and report to Commercial Street. Hale’s message brings them back.

## Scene 1 — Returning to the Lantern

**Place:** Front common room, rear yard and inn entrance. **Player purpose:** Establish what was found and preserve the accounts before accepting Arthur’s explanation.

A constable is keeping the yard clear. Hale remains with Arthur at the front, where the detectives obtain his account. Nora has already been taken to the mortuary. Baines sent George for a constable after he found her; Alden attended here, confirmed her death and requested further examination. Removal used a separate stretcher. The trunk and cart remain where they were found.

**Objects and documents:** Hale’s discovery account, including the scarf arrangement and the separate removal; discovery spot with little blood; closed trunk beside the service entrance; cart and coal sacks; recovered candlestick if inspected; the courtyard knife retained by Hale. No body or wound illustration appears.

| Player prompt or action | Response and resulting route |
| --- | --- |
| Ask Hale: “What happened while we were at the station?” | He explains discovery, attendance and removal, names Alden and the mortuary, and conveys Abberline’s assignment. This account opens the medical enquiry immediately. |
| Ask George: “Take us through your arrival.” | He came with the horse for the agreed job, left it outside and entered on foot. Baines asked him to help check on Nora. He found her before reaching the steps or moving the cart. He did not cover her face. |
| Press George: “You knew she would be here. Did you return during the night?” | He denies returning. His overnight cart arrangement can be checked with Baines. His denial is recorded as his account, not an eyewitness alibi. |
| Ask Arthur: “How are you related to Nora?” | “I’m her brother.” He requests her belongings and says he visited last night. Record the claim without granting him the trunk. |
| Ask Arthur: “What happened during your visit?” | He acknowledges an argument about Bristol and claims he left Nora alive, packing. He suggests George could have returned. |
| Ask Arthur: “Did you bring her trunk down?” | He denies touching it. This is available on the first interview, before any confrontation. |
| Open the trunk, then lift the loose clothes | Reveal bloodstained bedding and lining beneath them. Hale confirms the police did not use it during removal. |
| Ask Hale to keep the search private, then inspect the cart and lift a sack | Reveal the candlestick. Record its location and appearance. Identification and whoever hid it remain separate questions. |

Hale stays with Arthur at the front, with the rear passage shut. A separate constable guards the yard, and another officer checks Arthur’s lodging address without speaking to him. The yard search and George’s interview take place out of Arthur’s sight and hearing. Baines speaks in her own room, and neither witness visits Arthur before the station interview. Another officer accompanies George to his horse and back without taking him through the front room; the yard constable remains at the back steps. Hale’s account of these arrangements is collected before the sack can be lifted. Arthur is not shown the candlestick recovery record before he volunteers its location.

Baines’s initial interview gives her last ordinary contact with Nora: taking the candlestick to her room. She keeps to the lodgings and morning collection and deflects questions about the night. She did not admit or identify Arthur as a visitor. His visit is initially established by his own account; his claim to be Nora’s brother is made on returning that morning. Baines’s later account concerns an unidentified man, not a named suspect.

**Connections and routes:** Arthur’s denial of handling the trunk cannot be contradicted by an unidentified-man sighting. Keep it as his account to test later. The blood and bedding establish movement independently of the carrier’s identity. Hale’s account opens Alden’s destination. Baines can name Maggie’s workshop when asked who knows Nora; Nora’s correspondence offers another route to the same lead.

**Local completion:** Obtain the discovery account and inspect the trunk. Players may pursue the room, workshop, doctor or further inn interviews next. The overall enquiry stage remains open.

## Scene 2 — The journey she had prepared

**Place:** Nora’s room in the low rear extension. **Player purpose:** Reconstruct the disturbance and learn what Nora intended to leave behind.

Most of the packed clothes have been emptied beside the bed. A blanket is missing. Beneath the displaced rug is blood. Nora’s private possessions connect this scene to the workshop.

**Objects and documents:** Sewing case; torn wedding photograph with its date on the reverse; Bristol ticket in a handwritten sleeve addressed to **Nora Finch**; her sister’s job letter; work correspondence naming Maggie’s workshop; displaced clothes and rug; the candlestick’s empty place. Finch is Nora’s maiden name. The ordinary ticket establishes the journey; the addressed sleeve supplies the name clue.

| Player prompt or action | Response and resulting route |
| --- | --- |
| Open the sewing case and examine both sides of the photograph | Nora and her mother remain; the groom has been torn away. The date establishes a marriage, without identifying the missing man. |
| Read the travel sleeve and sister’s letter | Establish the Bristol journey under Finch and the job awaiting Nora. The name can be taken to Maggie before the photograph is examined. |
| Examine the clothes, bed and blood beneath the rug | Record the interrupted packing and signs of injury in this room. A follow-up with Baines identifies the trunk’s blanket as Nora’s. She explains where she last put it without assuming the detectives have examined the room. |
| Ask Baines: “What stood beside the bed?” | She identifies the missing candlestick by its bent handle and wax. If already found, the player can present it for confirmation immediately. |
| Follow the landing and inspect Baines’s window view | Establish the short route and what she could see. The washhouse blocks the discovery corner. This does not establish who used the route. |

**Connections:** Room blood/emptied packing + stained trunk interior support movement from room to yard. Baines’s bedding identification and the visible route provide the required context. Examining the torn photograph and travel sleeve gives players questions about the marriage and surname to take to Maggie; this is not another compulsory board connection.

**Local completion:** Record the room/trunk comparison and Nora’s travel plans. The photograph is available on this visit and remains recoverable during the later search stage. A direct, supported workshop route can establish the marriage without forcing players back for a reveal they already obtained.

## Scene 3 — The surgeon’s examination

**Place:** Workroom adjoining the mortuary’s examination room. **Player purpose:** Learn what the injuries establish and test the interpretation of the yard.

Alden expects the officers Hale mentioned. Nora remains beyond a closed door. He explains his findings calmly; access to them requires no hostile persuasion puzzle.

**Objects and documents:** Alden’s signed account after his explanation; optional presentation of the recovered candlestick; existing room, trunk and discovery records. No new physical clue appears in the examination room.

| Player prompt | Response and resulting route |
| --- | --- |
| “What caused her death?” | The blunt head injury was fatal. The throat and abdominal wounds were inflicted after death. This is the necessary medical account; it exposes the misleading knife left in the courtyard. |
| “Could she have been attacked in her room?” | With room/trunk evidence, Alden discusses how it supports movement. Without it, he asks what traces they found and leaves the comparison for a return visit. |
| “Could this candlestick have caused the head injury?” | On presentation, it is consistent with a hard, blunt blow. He cannot uniquely identify it as the weapon. A description of a missing object is not a forensic match. |
| “Does this make it another Whitechapel murder?” | He explains the resemblance and the limits: the injuries do not identify the same offender or establish deliberate imitation. |
| “Can you settle when she died?” | He cannot provide a precise time that decides the witness dispute. He establishes the head injury before the post-mortem knife wounds. Other timing comes from the inn accounts and physical evidence. |

The player can obtain the core findings first and leave, or pursue these comparisons in any order. Alden introduces the knife wounds himself. A separate question about Hale’s recovered knife requires the knife record, so an early mortuary visit never assumes an uncollected discovery. Alden’s written account records what he explained. Optional findings have separate follow-ups; nothing is silently added during another scene.

**Local completion:** Receive Alden’s cause-of-death and injury account. Optional object comparisons remain available later. The players can visit him before the workshop or detailed room examination.

## Scene 4 — Who Arthur really is

**Place:** Maggie’s garment workshop, investigated alongside Nora’s room. **Player purpose:** Establish the relationship Arthur concealed through questions and objects.

Maggie is working. Nora’s unfinished allocation is still there. Maggie first asks whether Arthur has come with the detectives, before disclosing the marriage or her part in Nora’s departure. She helped Nora leave and fears Arthur finding out. Maggie is Nora’s friend, not her sister or Arthur’s sister. Nora’s sister is in Bristol.

**Objects and documents:** Earlier work card under Nora Finch; Nora’s unfinished workshop work; Maggie’s complete wedding keepsake; the room’s torn picture, travel sleeve and sister’s letter if collected. The complete photograph stays with Maggie until requested and examined.

| Player prompt | Response and resulting route |
| --- | --- |
| “He hasn’t come with us. We can speak privately.” | Maggie asks them to close the door and agrees to speak. A separate optional work topic recalls Nora’s skill and concern about starting somewhere new. |
| “What are you afraid he’ll hear?” | “That I helped her. She asked me to keep quiet until she was away.” A direct question can also earn her cooperation; kindness is not the only correct button. |
| “Were you helping Nora hide from someone?” before establishing privacy | She asks whether Arthur is waiting outside and withholds detail. The private enquiry remains available; the player has not lost her evidence. |
| “We found Finch on her travelling papers. Is that her name?” | Maggie produces the earlier work card and explains Finch was her family name; she later used Vale. Her explanation also names Arthur as Nora’s husband and establishes the marriage without a repeated question. |
| “Was Arthur Vale her husband?” | Once willing to speak, Maggie answers truthfully, whether the players reached this through surname evidence or asked directly. She does not deny the relationship to preserve a puzzle. |
| “Do you have a photograph from the wedding?” | She hands over her complete print. Players examine it and recognize Arthur; her identification confirms the people and occasion. |
| “Why was she leaving him?” | Maggie gives a specific account of his control and Nora’s arrangements. She supplies the shared lodging address; Hale has another officer check it while he stays with Arthur. She does not claim to know who killed Nora. |

**Cooperative discovery:** One inspector can work through the room while the other questions Maggie. Comparing the earlier work card with the travel sleeve gives them a specific name question to ask her. On the board, the complete wedding photograph + Arthur’s brother claim establish the concealed marriage. The torn photograph suggests the husband question, but does not itself identify Arthur. Inspecting the complete print early is a valid alternative path.

All four explicit marriage routes—her answer about Arthur’s brother claim, the direct marriage question, the Finch explanation and identifying the complete photograph—set the same persistent marriage-recognition choice. Each keeps its own evidence source. The separation question and marriage deduction use that choice, so players never have to repeat a question already answered. The photograph remains separately required for the board comparison and Arthur’s challenge.

**Local completion:** Confirm the marriage and separation, learn the lodging address, and record why Maggie helped. Nora’s plans are clear. The search is still unavailable until the evidence of the disturbance and trunk movement supports it.

## Scene 5 — What the inn concealed

**Place:** Lantern common room, service landing and yard. **Player purpose:** Overcome Baines’s fear, establish what she actually observed and question the apparent connection with the Whitechapel murders.

Baines heard a disturbance from the rear rooms. Later, looking briefly from her window, she saw a man carrying Nora’s trunk down the steps. She could see the trunk and the movement, but not identify his face. She withdrew, bolted her door and hid. There was no face-to-face encounter or spoken threat. She feared the man might be the Whitechapel murderer; the discovery next morning strengthens that belief. This is her frightened interpretation, not an identification or a fact about the other murders.

Confronting her with blood in the trunk produces deflection: she lent Nora a candlestick; she does not look inside lodgers’ trunks. Evidence gives the detectives a reason to return to her account, but does not make her surrender it. They must discover that she fears the man may return, ask Hale to post a constable at the back steps, then return to reassure her and speak privately. Safety must be addressed before her fuller statement becomes available. Pressing or accusing her does not bypass that step; the player can recover by addressing the fear.

Once reassured, she gives the account in short exchanges: voices without recognisable words or speakers, a cry and a thud, later movement on the steps, the man with the trunk, and hiding behind her bolted door. She cannot identify Arthur by face, voice, clothing, a photograph or a later confrontation. She did not see the killing, the trunk’s contents or what the man did in the screened part of the yard. George also saw no one arriving or leaving. Hale remains with Arthur while the yard constable protects Baines; the protection lasts throughout the enquiry.

| Player prompt or action | Response and consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Present the blood in the room and trunk | She retreats into her duties and denies knowing what lodgers put in their luggage. No full account is awarded. |
| Ask what makes her afraid to speak | She reveals that she thinks the murderer may come back. The detectives learn what reassurance she needs. |
| Speak privately and arrange Hale’s protection | She starts to describe the disturbance and the unidentified man. Follow-ups establish each observation without supplying a name for her. |
| Ask whether it was Arthur | She cannot say. Neither pressure nor a photograph turns uncertainty into recognition. |
| Ask why she thinks it was the Whitechapel murderer | She refers to the fear in the district, what she heard and Nora’s discovery. The detectives distinguish her belief from what she saw. |
| Compare the man’s trunk movement with the stained bedding | Supports moving Nora from the room after the disturbance. It does not identify the carrier. |
| Ask why a supposedly roaming murderer would carry this lodger’s packed trunk out of her room | Opens the alternative explanation of an indoor attack followed by movement and concealment. The unusual behaviour challenges the apparent story; it cannot rule out the historical murderer by itself. |
| Present the candlestick to Baines | She can still identify the object she lent Nora. This ordinary object question does not require her full night account. |
| Question George about the cart | Access remains open to others overnight. Ownership of the cart does not identify who hid the candlestick. |
| Put the unidentified man and trunk to Arthur | He may maintain that it was someone else, or acknowledge helping with luggage while denying knowledge of blood. The detective must not claim Baines recognised him. |

**Connections:** Room traces + trunk bedding establish movement. Baines’s fuller account + the trunk evidence place that movement after the disturbance, with the room/trunk finding and window inspection as context. The reconstruction is cry/thud → later man carrying the trunk → Baines bolts her door and hides. It contains no named carrier, doorway threat or unseen knife blow.

**Joint completion of the enquiry stage:** Establish the indoor disturbance and movement, the marriage Arthur concealed, his admitted visit and disagreement, the medical findings, candlestick origin and cart access. Verify his former shared address through Hale, who relays another officer’s check with the landlord downstairs. Hale does not leave Arthur to make this check. These lead the enquiry towards his account and lodgings without asserting that Baines saw him carry Nora. The later interrogation tests Arthur’s knowledge of the private candlestick recovery. No identifying clasp, boot impression or clothing-fragment puzzle is required. Search progression must not require a named-carrier finding, an eyewitness contradiction of his trunk denial or evidence obtainable only in the search.

## Scene 6 — What he tried to burn

**Place:** Arthur’s lodgings. **Player purpose:** Examine the destroyed belongings and test the apparent knife killing.

Hale keeps Arthur at Commercial Street while the detectives search and is no longer an interview contact at the inn. His earlier accounts remain in the shared case. The cold hearth contains burned woollen clothing and a partly burned newspaper. There is no knife, sheath, clasp or matching room fragment. The knife is the one Hale recovered openly beside Nora in the courtyard; its owner is unknown.

The players separate the clothing and carefully unfold the surviving report. Both investigators must be present for the fragile paper. Its title, date and illustration identify another copy of the complete newspaper at the inn. The clothing is stained, but the game makes no laboratory identification of its wearer or the stains.

**Connections:** Burned report + complete newspaper establish the edition Arthur possessed. Courtyard knife + Alden’s findings establish that the apparent knife killing is misleading: the head injury was fatal, and the knife wounds came after death. Newspaper + Hale’s discovery account establish the scarf resemblance. These findings support staging as an inference without identifying its author.

**Joint completion:** Examine the clothing, recover the paper and complete those comparisons, then both choose **Return to Commercial Street**.

## Scene 7 — Arthur’s account

**Place:** Commercial Street interview room. **Player purpose:** Test Arthur’s account, notice his unshared knowledge and investigate his attempted explanation.

Either detective can conduct a topic. The other can listen, review the Case Map or take over between exchanges. Hale is available at the station for the source check. The conversation remains a series of playable exchanges.

### The marriage and initial denials

The marriage, trunk and burned-clothing questions can begin in any order. When told Maggie says he was Nora’s husband, Arthur asks who Maggie is and why she would say that. Repeating her claim does not produce an admission. The player must present Maggie’s complete wedding photograph; only then does Arthur acknowledge the marriage and complain that the detectives would have suspected him as soon as he said husband.

The trunk question follows the actual inn account: if he conceded carrying luggage, he denies knowing its contents; if he denied carrying it, he maintains that denial. Neither version identifies Baines’s unnamed carrier. The fire question concerns clothing and newspaper only.

Once the marriage is established, the detectives ask how Arthur found Nora. He says he waited outside the workshop on Friday and followed her to the Lantern without her knowledge. He learned she was going to Bristol when he went to her room. This account has its own short exchange and record before the detectives ask why he went to see Nora. He blames her refusal to listen and her sister’s encouragement. They put Nora’s own plans to him. Arthur initially says the argument ended with him leaving her unharmed and still packing. Baines’s cry and thud draw the reply that it could have happened afterwards.

### The candlestick slip

After those subjects, the detective says only: **“We’ve recovered the candlestick Mrs Baines lent Nora.”** No recovery exhibit is shown.

**Arthur:** Then why isn’t George sitting here?

**Investigator:** Why George?

**Arthur:** It was under the sacks in his cart. You ought to be asking him.

The exchange stops there. This records Arthur’s knowledge, not a confession. The player connects **Arthur’s statement + the candlestick recovery** on the Case Map. The finding identifies the accurate detail the detectives had not given him, with Hale’s private-search arrangements as required context.

Only after that connection can the detective ask who told him. Arthur says one of the officers did, cannot name the man and insists he was told rather than that he overheard it. The investigators must check that claim with Hale.

Hale accounts for the contacts: the removal officers left before Arthur arrived; Hale stayed with him at the front and escorted him to the station. The yard constable and the officer checking the lodging address kept away from Arthur and did not discuss discoveries within his hearing. He did not mention the candlestick. George was interviewed in the yard and accompanied to his horse and back by another officer; Baines spoke in her own room. Neither visited Arthur. The detectives know what they themselves said in the earlier playable exchanges.

The player then connects **Arthur’s claimed source + Hale’s check**. This unlocks the rebuttal. Arthur answers that Hale has forgotten, or repeats that he cannot remember every conversation. He never helpfully identifies another witness who would confirm his excuse.

The hiding place must stay unshared on every route. Do not put it in an earlier investigator line to Arthur, an exhibit shown before the slip, or a witness conversation in his hearing. The search can be approached before or after other enquiries, but lifting the sack requires Hale’s privacy arrangement first.

### Medical findings, newspaper and optional concessions

The detective puts the medical finding to him: the head injury killed Nora and the knife wounds were made after death. Arthur denies striking her. The newspaper gets an ordinary explanation—everyone was reading about the murders, and he had finished with his copy. Its presence does not force an admission of imitation.

The case is reportable once the principal accounts, slip, source check, medical and newspaper allegations have been addressed. The joint conclusion rests on the physical reconstruction plus Arthur’s concealed marriage, controlling conduct, admitted visit and argument, unexplained knowledge and changing answers. It does not label his slip a complete confession.

Players may continue. Presented with the disturbed room and Baines’s account, Arthur can retreat to claiming that he took Nora’s arm, she pulled away and fell against the washstand. Showing the trunk evidence can then draw out moving her and covering her face, justified as fear. The final newspaper challenge can expose his hope that the detectives would blame the other murderer. He refuses to discuss the knife and continues to say she fell. His final answer is **“I didn’t kill her.”**

A bare accusation can produce a recoverable refusal. The optional final disguise exchange closes the interview after its last line, then opens the joint report. No further accounting of each wound or concealed object follows. Both players still have to agree; opening the report never submits it.

## Closing — The same table

**Place:** Lantern common room. **Player purpose:** Decide what to say to the people left with Nora’s absence.

An urgent police message was sent to Bristol when the search was arranged. Maggie is preparing a personal letter to Nora’s sister, who should already have received word of the death. The newspaper is set aside while Maggie writes. The letter provides the account of someone who knew Nora here; it does not attempt to prevent a station wait that has already happened. Nora should have reached Bristol earlier that Saturday.

| Player prompt | Response and consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Ask Maggie: “Will you write to her sister?” | She explains what she intends to send and asks about Nora’s belongings. The reply concerns the completed enquiry, without inventing a confession that was never given. |

Speaking to Maggie makes **Close the enquiry** available. Both players must agree to finish. End on the common room continuing around the place where Nora worked. No additional suspect, conspiracy or speech from Arthur follows.

## Framework mapping for authoring

This script uses the existing [story package](story-authoring.md), [interview](interviews.md), [Case Map](case-map.md) and [progression](framework.md) contracts. The following mappings are part of the script’s implementation requirements, not new features claimed to exist.

| Script behavior | Existing mechanism and authoring requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Select a question, then choose an approach or exhibit | Interview `topics`, fully worded `choices`, `proof` and `proofAlternatives`. Use one choice per approach; more questions become topics. The player selects an actual evidence record when a challenge requires one. |
| Two useful approaches produce different accounts | Authored `responses` can mark either response successful and give different text, `choice` values and follow-ups. Keep the common enquiry record available on each valid route. |
| A witness avoids a question; the player returns with support | `repeatable` interviews, `requiresAll`, `requiresDeduction` and `afterUnsuccessfulTopic`. A successful reply cannot be rewritten by retrying; author a new topic to challenge it. |
| Arthur changes an earlier denial into an accident account | The optional room-revisit topic awards its own testimony after the required accounts. Earlier successful answers remain in the record. |
| Finish a subject after a recorded denial | Award a neutral record such as “Arthur’s account of the yard”, meaning he was questioned. The saved reply contains the actual denial or admission. ### Representative playable routes

- **Objects first:** Inspect the trunk, room and cart; ask Baines to identify the blanket and candlestick; take the name discrepancy to Maggie; visit Alden; reconstruct the disturbance; search; challenge Arthur with the recovered objects.
- **Witnesses first:** Hear Hale, Baines and Arthur; visit Maggie directly through Baines’s lead; confirm the marriage without needing the torn picture first; collect room evidence; visit Alden; return to test the trunk accounts; search; question Arthur about Nora’s plans.
- **A mistaken suspicion:** Find the candlestick in George’s cart and accuse him; obtain the overnight-access explanation; distinguish Baines’s unidentified-man account from Arthur’s denial; recover the enquiry without losing evidence or excluding later partial admissions.
- **An evidence-based ending without a confession:** Put the principal subjects to Arthur, notice his slip and check the source claim, record his denials or partial admissions, and jointly submit the supported case. Skip the optional movement and staging follow-ups by choice; the same murder is solved, with a different recorded interview.

## Private sequence and continuity

This is the author’s sequence. Players reconstruct only what their evidence supports; alternative dialogue routes do not change these events.

1. Nora leaves Arthur and lodges alone at the Lantern. She plans sewing work near her sister in Bristol, using Finch again. She tears her own wedding photograph; Maggie keeps her complete copy.
2. Hale reserves front rooms for Reed and Ellis without revealing their occupation. They arrive, meet Nora and learn the luggage arrangement. George leaves his coal cart in the yard for the morning job. The detectives retire before Arthur arrives.
3. Earlier on Friday Arthur waits outside the workshop and follows Nora to the Lantern without her knowledge. After the detectives retire, he reaches her room without Baines or George witnessing his arrival. Nora tells him about Bristol during this visit. Nora has not invited him. George takes the horse to its stable without noticing anyone coming or going; the cart remains. Arthur’s claim to be her brother comes the next morning, not in an entrance conversation with Baines.
4. Nora refuses to return. Arthur strikes her with the heavy candlestick. The blunt head injury kills her.
5. Baines hears voices, a cry and a thud from the rear rooms. She cannot recognise the speakers or make out the words. She does not open her door or confront anyone. The inspectors, asleep at the front beyond the common room and service passage, hear none of it.
6. Arthur obtains an ordinary knife and inflicts post-mortem injuries to imitate accounts he read in his own newspaper. He did not arrive wearing a knife in a sheath. The playable enquiry does not establish the knife’s owner. He covers visible blood with the rug, empties most of the travelling clothes and uses the bedding and trunk to move her into the yard.
7. Baines hears movement and looks briefly from her window. She sees a man carrying Nora’s trunk down the steps but cannot identify him. In the private sequence he is Arthur; her evidence never establishes that. Fearing the Whitechapel murderer, she withdraws, bolts her door and hides. The projecting washhouse screens the discovery corner. She sees neither the contents nor what he does there.
8. Arthur removes Nora from the trunk, arranges the scarf and leaves her in the screened corner with the knife openly beside her, suggesting it caused her death. He conceals the candlestick beneath sacks in George’s cart. He leaves the trunk by the service entrance with a few clothes over the stained bedding. He expects his claimed relationship to let him take it later.
9. At his lodgings Arthur burns stained clothing and his newspaper. He changes clothes but has not cleared the hearth before returning to the inn. The complete inn newspaper never left its common room.
10. Reed and Ellis leave through the front entrance to report to Commercial Street. George later arrives with the horse, enters on foot and, at Baines’s request, goes to check on Nora. He finds her before reaching the steps or moving the cart and fetches Hale. He does not move the scarf.
11. Hale records the discovery, recovers and retains the knife, summons Alden and secures the scene. After Alden attends, Hale arranges removal by a separate stretcher and sends word to Commercial Street. The trunk, cart and their contents remain for examination. The detectives return after the body has gone and identify themselves as police.
12. Arthur presents himself as Nora’s brother, seeks her belongings, admits the visit and disagreement, denies handling the trunk and suggests George. The players establish the concealed marriage, indoor disturbance and movement by an unidentified man, then obtain the arranged search. Hale remains with Arthur at the front during the private yard search and witness interviews, then escorts him to the station and stays there during the lodging search. Other officers guard the yard, protect Baines and check the address without speaking to Arthur. An urgent message concerning Nora’s death is sent to Bristol when the search is arranged. No recovery location is disclosed to him.
13. The search recovers burned clothing and the report. At the station Arthur volunteers the candlestick’s hiding place while blaming George, then falsely claims an officer told him. The players compare and check those statements. The fuller route obtains limited admissions to movement and disguise, but Arthur keeps denying the fatal blow. The enquiry closes with the people at the inn and Nora’s interrupted journey.

The trunk is large enough for its role and follows a short service landing with a few broad steps, not a full staircase. The set must preserve these dimensions and sightlines. Baines did not see the killing or identify the man with the trunk. Her fear of the Whitechapel murderer is an interpretation, not corroboration of his involvement. George’s account is not a witnessed alibi for the whole night. Alden cannot uniquely identify the weapons or read intent from an injury. Newspaper ownership alone proves neither murder nor imitation. The public report and scarf illustration are authored fiction, not claims about an actual issue’s contents.
