# Character dress, November 1888

The live 3D cast uses restrained late-Victorian day clothes. These are interpretations for fictional people, not reconstructions of documented individuals. Keep the existing dossier portraits as facial and palette references. Clothing must communicate work, means and habit without suggesting guilt.

## Research

- [FIT, 1880–1889](https://fashionhistory.fitnyc.edu/1880-1889/): fitted day bodices, long skirts, waistcoats and the distinctions between informal lounge suits and formal coats. Use the decade's silhouette without giving working women elaborate fashionable trimmings.
- [V&A, corsets, crinolines and bustles](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/corsets-crinolines-and-bustles-fashionable-victorian-underwear): skirt support changed through the century, with the pronounced bustle falling out of favour in the late 1880s. Use modest gathered backs, not enormous hoops or projecting shelves.
- [London Museum, Street life and work in 1877](https://www.londonmuseum.org.uk/collections/london-stories/street-life-work-1877-john-thomson/): photographic reference for practical layered clothing and occupational differences. These photographs predate the story by eleven years; use them for working dress rather than precise 1888 fashion dating.
- [V&A, Hair](https://www.vam.ac.uk/blog/design-and-society/pandemic-objects-hair): long hair was dressed at home, with paid help available to affluent women. The cast's pinned coils and plaits are practical interpretations of long hair, not cropped haircuts or modern loose salon styles.
- [Metropolitan Police, historical timeline](https://www.met.police.uk/police-forces/metropolitan-police/areas/about-us/about-the-met/met-museums-archives/timeline/): helmets and tunics replaced uniform top hats and tailcoats in 1863. Hale wears a closed blue tunic and domed helmet. His small light metal fittings are stylized; the model does not claim an exact divisional badge reconstruction.
